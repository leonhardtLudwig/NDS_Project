The comparison answers the three questions of the introduction. Whether the opinions
converge: a self-weight makes the FDG model converge only by making the network
aperiodic, whereas a prejudice that influences every agent makes the FJ model
asymptotically stable on any network, periodic ones included
(Figures~\ref{fig:fdg-conv} and~\ref{fig:fj-one-prejudiced}). What they converge to: on a
strongly connected network with a self-weight the FDG model always reaches a consensus,
whereas the final opinions of the FJ model are fixed by the prejudices and, when all
agents are P-dependent, coincide only if all the prejudiced agents share the same
prejudice (Figures~\ref{fig:fj-one-prejudiced} and~\ref{fig:fj-all-prejudiced}). How
much each agent weighs: the social power of agent~$1$ grows gradually with its
self-weight, from $1/6$ to $1$, whereas a single anchor takes the whole influence
centrality, and the two measures meet in the limit of Lemma~\ref{lem:centrality-limit}
(Figures~\ref{fig:fdg-power} and~\ref{fig:fj-lemma24}).
