\paragraph{Stubborn agents in the two models.}
A stubborn agent of the FJ model is an agent with $\lambda_i = 0$ and $u_i = x_i(0)$
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:fj-model}). As in the FDG model, stubbornness is the extreme
case of a graded mechanism, here the prejudice: an agent with $0 < \lambda_i < 1$ is
prejudiced but not stubborn, since its opinion moves, even
if~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial} informally calls prejudiced agents ``partially''
stubborn. Unlike the FDG model, where $a_{ii} = 1$ suffices, a condition on the matrix
only, here $\lambda_i = 0$ alone does not: stubbornness also requires $u_i = x_i(0)$, a
condition on the input.

\paragraph{From one model to the other.}
The FJ model and the FDG model with stubborn agents can be translated into each other.
In one direction, Section~\ref{subsubsec:fj-final} has shown,
following~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial}, that an FJ model is an FDG model on an
augmented graph, in which every prejudice becomes a virtual stubborn agent
(Figure~\ref{fig:augmented}). In the other direction, an FDG model with stubborn agents
can be written as an FJ model in two ways: the rewriting
of~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial}, which keeps each stubborn agent as an agent of the FJ
model, and a rewriting of ours, which removes it and turns it into the prejudice of the
agents that listen to it. The second one is the augmented graph read backwards. The two
rewritings are presented in this order.

\paragraph{State or input.}
The two models of Figure~\ref{fig:fj-stub-equivalence} have the same trajectories, but
not the same stability. The FDG model with stubborn agents converges under the
following condition.

\begin{corollary}[\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial}]
\label{cor:fdg-stubborn}
Let the group have $s \geq 1$ stubborn agents, influencing all other individuals
(i.e.\ every other node of $G$ is connected by a walk to the set of stubborn nodes).
Then the FDG model~\eqref{eq:fdg} is convergent.
\end{corollary}

% stubborn agents are "source nodes" and "the set of source nodes is connected by walks
% to all other nodes of G[W]"; with the convention of this report (i -> j means that i
% listens to j) the walks go from the other nodes to the stubborn ones. "model (2)" of
% the tutorial is the FDG model eq:fdg.

Under the assumption of Corollary~\ref{cor:fdg-stubborn}, every agent of the FJ model
obtained by the rewriting is prejudiced or influenced by a prejudiced agent, so the FJ
model is asymptotically stable (Corollary~\ref{cor:fj-stable}), whereas the FDG model
is convergent but not asymptotically stable, since a row-stochastic matrix always has
the eigenvalue $1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:stoch-spectrum}, see
also~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial}). The explanation given
in~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial} is that changing the initial condition $x(0)$ of the
FJ model leaves the prejudice vector $u$ unchanged, while in the FDG model the prejudice
is part of the state vector.

Figure~\ref{fig:fj-stub-perturbation} starts the two models again from $x_1(0) = 0.5$,
leaving $u$ unchanged: now $u_1 = 0 \neq x_1(0)$, and agent~$1$ of the FJ model is
prejudiced but no longer stubborn.

The difference $\delta(k)$ shows where the perturbation of $x_1(0)$ is at each step.
In the FJ model, subtracting the two equations~\eqref{eq:fj} written for $\tilde x$ and
for $x$, the term $(I - \Lambda)u$ cancels, since the prejudices are the same, and
(this computation is ours)
\[
  \delta(k+1) = \Lambda W\, \delta(k).
\]
This is the same recursion as~\eqref{eq:fj-error}: both $\delta(k)$ and $e(k)$ are
differences of two solutions with the same $u$, the second one being, for $e(k)$, the
constant solution $x(\infty)$. The two quantities, however, measure different things:
$e(k)$ tells how far a single trajectory still is from its limit, $\delta(k)$ how much
of the perturbation is still present.

Since all agents are P-dependent, $\Lambda W$ is
Schur stable (Corollary~\ref{cor:fj-stable}) and $\delta(k) \to 0$: the perturbation
is forgotten. On the directed cycle it travels backwards along the cycle, from
agent~$1$ to agents $6, 5, \dots, 2$, and when it comes back to agent~$1$ it is
cancelled, since $\lambda_1 = 0$: $\delta(k) = 0$ for $k \geq 6$. On the bidirectional
ring it is split between the neighbours and fades gradually. In the FDG model there is
no input to cancel, and the same computation gives $\delta(k+1) = A\,\delta(k)$ (ours).
Agent~$1$ is the only globally reachable node, with social power $w = e_1$, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:consensus} $\delta(k) \to (w^\top \delta(0))\mathbf{1}_6 =
0.5\,\mathbf{1}_6$: the stubborn agent keeps the perturbation and passes it to
everybody.

\paragraph{The stubborn agent as a prejudice of its neighbours.}

\[

           = \tfrac{1}{2}\, x_5(k) + \tfrac{1}{2}\, x_1(0),

$u_6 = x_1(0)$ and influence weight $1$ on agent~$5$; the same holds for agent~$2$.

In the FDG model, $A$ is primitive (Corollary~\ref{cor:primitive-selfloop}) and
doubly-stochastic, so the group agrees on the average $0.5$ of the initial opinions
(Corollary~\ref{cor:average}): the self-weights slow the convergence down, but do not
prevent the consensus. In the FJ model every agent is prejudiced ($\Lambda < I_6$), so
the model is asymptotically stable (Corollary~\ref{cor:fj-stable}) and $x(\infty) = Vu$,
with $V = 0.2\,(I - 0.8\,W)^{-1}$. The matrix $V$ is stochastic
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fj-general}): every final opinion is a convex combination of the
prejudices, and $v_{ij}$ is the weight of $u_j$ in $x_i(\infty)$. No column of $V$ is
zero, so every prejudice enters every final opinion, but with weights that change from
row to row: the final opinions are closer to each other than the initial ones, but
different. A consensus requires equal prejudices: since $V$ is invertible and
$V\mathbf{1}_6 = \mathbf{1}_6$, $Vu = c\,\mathbf{1}_6$ only for $u = c\,\mathbf{1}_6$
(this remark is ours).

This is the main difference between the two models. On a strongly connected network the
FDG model can only reach a consensus or not converge (cases (i)--(ii) of
Table~\ref{tab:rosetta}), since a disagreement needs at least two closed groups
(Theorem~\ref{thm:disagreement}); the FJ model disagrees on the same network, so its
disagreement comes from the prejudices. In the augmented graph
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:fj-final}, our reading) the six prejudices are six stubborn
virtual agents with different opinions, as in Remark~\ref{rem:many-stubborn}. In social
terms, inertia gives way in the end, an anchor does not: a group anchored to different
opinions settles on a persistent disagreement, a cleavage of opinions in the words
of~\cite{proskurnikov2017tutorial}.

\end{itemize}
In social terms, a prejudice acts only on the agents that listen, directly or through
intermediaries, to the prejudiced agent: when it reaches everybody, the FJ model is
asymptotically stable and the structure of the network does not matter; when it does
not, the agents it does not reach form an FDG model of their own, which needs
aperiodicity to converge and remembers their initial opinions.
